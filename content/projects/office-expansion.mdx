## The problem

The practice needed more workspace. IT's part of that is rarely the visible part: it is the cabling schedule, the port count, the switching capacity and the assurance that every desk actually works on the first morning.

The constraint was the same one that shapes most of this environment — no dedicated project team. The work had to happen in parallel with day-to-day support, using external contractors where the skills genuinely sat outside the department.

## What I did

I started with a **port schedule rather than a cable order**: how many desks, where, how many devices per desk, and what the patch panel and switch would need to look like to keep changes sane later.

From there I gathered vendor quotations against the same specification, compared them on scope rather than headline price, and coordinated the cabling works so the physical build matched the network design. Switch ports were allocated and labelled to the same scheme as the existing floor, and each workspace was tested before handover rather than after someone sat down at it.

The work was scheduled to keep the existing floor running: no shared uplink was taken down during working hours.

## What changed

- Additional workspace with tested connectivity on day one.
- A documented port allocation that matches the physical labelling.
- Vendor scope and cost recorded as part of the CAPEX process.
- The same cabling and addressing conventions as the existing floor, so the next expansion starts from a plan instead of a guess.

## What I would do next

- Keep an as-built drawing with the port schedule, updated whenever a run changes.
- Add a spare-port margin to future builds; expansions rarely arrive at the planned size.
